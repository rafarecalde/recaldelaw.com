Moving a PPLI Policy from an LLC into an ILIT

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

Some families already hold a private placement life insurance contract inside an LLC. The members are one or both spouses. The planning step is to move that ownership into an irrevocable life insurance trust. What the contract is, and the conditions in sections 7702 and 817(h) and the investor-control authorities, stay on the PPLI page, in PPLI as an investment wrapper, and in PPLI in estate and income tax planning. This note is only the move.

The gift is of the units

The ILIT becomes the member. The gift is an assignment of LLC units, made in the way the operating agreement requires, with the member register brought current and any consent the agreement demands actually signed. The gift-tax value is the value of the units transferred. That value is an appraisal of the interest given, which reflects the policy the LLC holds. It is not, by itself, the death benefit. The death benefit is the estate-tax figure if section 2042 still applies at death. The tax adviser decides which number goes on the gift-tax return. Section 101(a)(2) is the transfer-for-value rule. A pure gift of the units, or a transfer to an ILIT that is a grantor trust as to the insured, generally stays within the exceptions, and the death benefit can remain excluded from income. A part-sale, or a buyout of a member's units, can make the death benefit income-taxable. The tax adviser should check that before anyone signs.

A gift of a slice that leaves the insured in control of the LLC has not moved the incidents of ownership. The incidents are the powers over the contract: to change the beneficiary, to surrender, to assign, to pledge, to borrow. If the insured can still cause the LLC to do those things, section 2042 still has a person to point to.

The insured spouse has no role

The insured resigns as manager, and as any officer or authorized signer the carrier or the operating agreement recognizes, as part of the same transfer. The insured does not become trustee of the ILIT. The trustee of the ILIT, who is not the insured, is the member who can direct the LLC, or the LLC is managed by that trustee. The other spouse may be a beneficiary of the ILIT. Being a beneficiary is a right to receive what the instrument directs. It is not a power to deal with the carrier.

Where both spouses were members, each gift is documented. A spouse who is not the insured can be the donor of units he or she owned. The insured, if he or she owned units, is the donor of those units and parts with them. Community-property character, if any, is read before anyone assumes one signature is enough. The firm's note on Florida community property trusts is a different regime. An LLC operating agreement is not a community property trust.

The three-year rule

Section 2035 includes in the gross estate certain transfers made within three years of death, including a transfer of an interest that would have been included under section 2042 if the decedent had kept it. For a policy the insured effectively owned through the LLC, the three years run from the transfer that gives up the incidents. Death inside that period can bring the proceeds into the estate, not merely the gift-tax value of the units.

A transfer that leaves a manager role, a veto, or a power to change the beneficiary behind has not started a clean period, because the incidents have not left. A policy the trustee of the ILIT applies for from the beginning, which the insured never owns, is the other path, described in life insurance and the $15 million exemption. Changing the owner of an existing contract does not answer investor control. That question stays with the carrier and the tax adviser.
