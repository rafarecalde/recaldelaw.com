A Wyoming Irrevocable Trust When You Live in Florida

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

A family can live in Florida and still have an irrevocable trust governed by Wyoming law. Residence and governing law are different facts. A sentence in a Florida instrument that names Wyoming law does not, by itself, meet a Wyoming statute that requires a trustee with a Wyoming connection or administration in Wyoming. The trustee, or a Wyoming private trust company, is what supplies that connection. The company is described, from the Florida family's side, in a Wyoming private trust company for a Florida family, and the statute is in Wyoming private trust companies.

Duration

Wyoming Statutes section 34-1-139 permits a trust created after July 1, 2003 to last up to 1,000 years when the trust holds property other than real property and the statute's other conditions are met, including Wyoming governing law and a trustee with the Wyoming connection the statute requires. Section 34-1-139(b)(v) requires that the trust terms themselves make every interest, and every power of appointment, vest or terminate within those 1,000 years. A Florida-drafted form may not say that. The Wyoming instrument has to. An interest in real property is under the real-property rule. A long-term trust often owns an LLC that owns the land, so the trust's asset is the membership interest.

Florida has its own period. Under section 689.225, a trust created on or after July 1, 2022 may use 1,000 years in place of the 90-year period, unless the instrument requires an earlier end. A trust created after 2000 and before that date may use 360 years. A Florida family signing a new trust in 2026 can have a long Florida trust. Duration, by itself, is a weak reason to choose Wyoming for a trust created now. The Wyoming statute still matters when the family wants Wyoming administration and the real-property distinction is handled through an entity.

Directed trusts

Wyoming law lets the instrument divide investment decisions and distribution decisions from the trustee who keeps the records. Florida has the same kind of statute. The Florida Uniform Directed Trust Act is Part XIV of Chapter 736, beginning at section 736.1401. A Florida family can use a directed trust under Florida law, with a Florida administrative trustee. Wyoming is the choice when the family also wants a Wyoming trustee and the rest of that state's fiduciary law. The roles, and what the grantor can hold, are in directed trusts.

Privacy

A trust instrument is not filed in a probate proceeding the way a will is. That is true of a Florida trust that is funded, and it is true of a Wyoming trust. Beneficiaries still have the information rights the governing statute gives them. Privacy here means the dispositive instrument is not a public court file. It does not mean the trustee keeps the trust secret from the people it benefits.

Asset protection

Wyoming law provides a qualified spendthrift trust of which the settlor can be a beneficiary, if the statute's conditions are met, including a qualified trustee with a Wyoming connection. A Florida resident remains subject to Florida creditor law. Choosing Wyoming law does not decide how a Florida court will treat a transfer the settlor can still benefit from. The transfer still has to be a real transfer. The voidable-transaction rules of the states involved still apply. We describe the statute. Where a creditor claim is already live, that question belongs with litigation counsel as well as with the tax adviser.

The federal estate-tax design is independent of which state's trust code is chosen. A retained power to enjoy the property is still read under sections 2036 and 2038. For 2026 the basic exclusion is $15 million per person, indexed afterward. Situs does not change that figure.
