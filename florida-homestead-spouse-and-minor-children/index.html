Florida Homestead, a Spouse, and Minor Children

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

A revocable trust often says the surviving spouse receives the house. When the house is Florida homestead and a minor child also survives the owner, that sentence does not control the homestead. This note is that descent result. The constitutional limit, the lifetime deed, and the spouse's joinder on a transfer are already in Florida homestead and transfers into a trust, including for owners who are not U.S. persons. The Spanish note of that article is homestead en Florida y la transferencia a un Trust. Those pages do not restate the descent that follows a devise the constitution does not allow. This one does, for a Florida family whose home is in a revocable trust.

The trust's direction is a devise

Article X, section 4(c) of the Florida Constitution provides that the homestead shall not be subject to devise if the owner is survived by a spouse or a minor child, except that it may be devised to the owner's spouse if there is no minor child. Section 732.4015 states the same limit. For that purpose the owner includes the grantor of a trust described in section 733.707(3) that exists at death, and a disposition by that trust of the homestead is a devise. A revocable living trust is that kind of trust. The paragraph that leaves the house to the spouse, when a minor child survives, is a devise the constitution does not allow. When no minor child survives, the homestead may be devised to the spouse. That neighboring case is the one the existing note already states.

What descent does instead

Section 732.401(1) provides that homestead not devised as the law and the constitution allow descends as other intestate property, with one override. If a spouse and one or more descendants survive, the spouse takes a life estate, and the descendants living at the death take a vested remainder, per stirpes. The minor child is one of those descendants. Adult children of the owner, including children of an earlier relationship who survive, are descendants too.

Section 732.401(2) lets the surviving spouse elect, instead of the life estate, an undivided one-half interest as a tenant in common. The other half vests in the descendants living at the death, per stirpes. The election is made within six months after the death and during the spouse's lifetime. It is made by a notice recorded in the county where the homestead is. Once made, it is irrevocable. A guardian of the spouse's property, or an attorney-in-fact, may make the election only with approval of the court that has jurisdiction of the real property. The petition for that approval is filed within the same six months, and a timely petition extends the time to elect until at least 30 days after the court's order. Until it is made, the spouse is the life tenant, and carrying costs are allocated with the remaindermen under chapter 738.

The trustee of the revocable trust does not deed the homestead according to the failed instruction. Title follows the statute. A sale or mortgage of a minor's remainder generally requires the authority Florida law provides for a minor's real property, which can include a guardian of the property and a court. The personal representative and the trustee coordinate that proceeding. They do not replace it with a deed signed under the trust.

Title that never reaches this statute

Section 732.401(5) provides that the descent section does not apply to property the decedent owned as tenants by the entireties or in joint tenancy with right of survivorship. How the home is titled is the first fact. A home in tenancy by the entireties may pass to the spouse by survivorship. That survivorship is outside section 732.401. Putting the home into the revocable trust during life is a conveyance, and for homestead it needs the spouse's joinder, which is the subject of the existing note.

The rest of the trust still governs property that is not homestead. A failed homestead devise does not revoke the trust. A spouse may waive homestead rights in the manner Florida law allows. A minor child cannot. Any waiver is drafted to the statute. The federal estate tax is a separate computation. Homestead descent does not decide whether the home is in the gross estate. For 2026 the basic exclusion is $15 million per person, indexed afterward. The basis consequence of leaving an asset in the estate, or of giving it away, is covered in step-up in basis and the swap power.
