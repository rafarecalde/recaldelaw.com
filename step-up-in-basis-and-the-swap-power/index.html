Step-Up in Basis and the Swap Power

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

Property included in the gross estate generally takes a basis equal to its fair market value at death, under section 1014. The family's built-in gain disappears. Property given to an irrevocable trust, and kept outside the estate, generally keeps the donor's basis under section 1015. The estate-tax removal and the basis adjustment are two descriptions of the same fact. You receive one by giving up the other, unless something brings the asset back into the estate before death.

A sale to a grantor trust, compared with a gift, is covered in selling to an intentionally defective grantor trust. That note makes the point that neither pattern, by itself, preserves a section 1014 adjustment in the asset that left. This note is about the power that can bring a chosen asset back.

What stays behind in the trust

The donee's gain on a later sale is measured from the donor's basis. For a long-held portfolio, or for the vacation rental in the LLC example, that gain can be large. Depreciation already taken can be part of the computation. The family has exchanged a possible estate tax on the asset, at whatever the exclusion and the rate are in the year of death, for an income tax on the appreciation when someone sells. For 2026 the basic exclusion is $15 million per person, indexed afterward. A family under that line may care more about basis than about estate tax. A family over it has both numbers, and the tax adviser compares them before the gift is made.

The substitution power

Section 675(4)(C) treats the grantor as the income-tax owner of a trust when a power to reacquire the trust corpus by substituting other property of an equivalent value is exercisable in a nonfiduciary capacity. That is the income-tax half. The grantor reports the trust's income, which is the grantor-trust result the sale-versus-gift note describes.

The estate-tax half is separate. A power that is only the substitution of equivalent value, with the trustee obligated to confirm that the values match, is the power used so the power itself does not become a retained right to income or a power to shift benefits among beneficiaries under sections 2036 and 2038. Revenue Ruling 2008-22 is the ruling the tax adviser compares the instrument to. The grantor exercises the power in a nonfiduciary capacity. The trustee, acting as a fiduciary, confirms equivalence. The power is not a way to prefer one beneficiary over another. A broader power to decide who enjoys the property is a different power, and it is read under sections 2036 and 2038 on its own terms.

Section 736.1405(2)(e) keeps that power outside Florida's directed-trust statute when the trust instrument expressly provides that the power is held in a nonfiduciary capacity, as needed for the settlor's tax objectives. The swap power is written that way. It is not the distribution adviser's job. Directed roles are covered in directed trusts.

How the step-up is recovered

While the grantor is able to act, the grantor transfers cash or other high-basis assets to the trust and takes back the low-basis asset, at equal value. The trustee's confirmation of value is part of the file, and an appraisal is part of it when the asset is hard to price. The low-basis asset is then in the grantor's estate. At death, section 1014 can adjust its basis. The trust keeps the substitute. The substitute stays outside the estate and does not receive that adjustment.

The power does not create a step-up by sitting in the instrument. The inclusion of the asset the grantor actually reacquired creates it. If the power is never exercised, the low-basis asset remains in the trust on the old basis. An unequal exchange is a gift, or a retained string, depending on the direction of the difference. A swap of a policy on the grantor's own life is a different question, because taking the policy back can restore incidents of ownership under section 2042. Revenue Ruling 2011-28 is the ruling the tax adviser reads for a policy. The ownership rules are in why a policy gets its own trust.
