What a trust does for the family

A trust is a way to hold property, name who manages it, and say who receives it. Families use one when they have children, when they buy a Florida home, and when they own a company. The useful moment is often while children are still minors.

Probate

Property titled in the trustee’s name is administered under the trust. Property still titled in your individual name at death is generally a Florida probate asset. After a death, Florida law requires whoever holds the original will to deposit it with the court clerk, so the will becomes a court record even if no probate is opened.

Privacy

A funded trust’s terms stay in the family’s documents. After a death, the trustee files a short notice of trust with the court. The notice names the trust and the trustee. The trustee still has to keep the beneficiaries informed.

Children and minors

A trust can hold what a child inherits and pay it out for health, education, maintenance, and support, or at ages you choose. An inheritance paid outright at 18 arrives at 18. The trust is the document that can say otherwise. Parents can receive and manage up to $15,000 left to a minor. The trustee manages a larger inheritance held in the trust.

Guardianship

Parents nominate the person they want to raise a minor child, in the will or in a written declaration. A Florida written declaration is signed with two witnesses and filed with the court clerk. A Florida court appoints the guardian. The trust holds property for the child. The trust does not make the appointment.

Incapacity

If you cannot manage your affairs, the successor trustee manages what is already in the trust. A durable power of attorney names an agent for assets still in your name. An agent can change your trust only if two things are true: you initialed or signed next to that specific power in the power of attorney, and the trust itself expressly allows your agent to amend or revoke it. Medical decisions sit in a designation of health care surrogate and, if you want one, a living will.

Blended families

A trust can provide for a spouse and direct the remainder to children from an earlier relationship, for property the trust is allowed to control. Florida homestead follows its own limits when a spouse or a minor child survives, which the homestead notes below describe.

Real estate and homestead

A Florida home can be deeded to the trustee. A deed of homestead by a married owner needs the spouse’s joinder. A deed of the home into your revocable trust keeps the Florida homestead property tax exemption when the trust gives you the right to live there. If the owner dies survived by a spouse or a minor child, the Florida Constitution limits who can receive the homestead. A sentence in a revocable trust that leaves the house to the spouse does not control when a minor child also survives. See Florida homestead and transfers into a trust and Florida homestead, a spouse, and minor children.

Business interests

Stock and LLC interests can be assigned to the trustee when the company’s documents allow that transfer. Until the company recognizes it, the interest is still in the owner’s name. The trust can name who handles the interest if the owner dies or cannot act, to the extent those documents let the trustee step in.

The documents, in plain English

Most Florida families start with a revocable living trust and the papers around it. An irrevocable trust is a different tool, used when the plan includes a completed gift. We draft the legal structure. Your tax adviser confirms the tax result.

Revocable living trust

You can change or revoke it while you are alive and have capacity, unless the document says otherwise. You can serve as trustee and keep using the house and the accounts. For federal estate tax, property you can still revoke, and property whose income you still receive, are generally included in your estate. Sections 2036 and 2038 are the provisions the tax adviser applies. At your death, you can no longer revoke it. While you are living, it is commonly reported under your Social Security number. Amending one later, without creating a new owner, is covered in amending or restating a revocable trust.

Some married couples also use a Florida community property trust, available for trusts signed on or after July 1, 2021. The goal is a new tax basis for both halves of the property at the first death. The IRS has not confirmed that this works for an elective Florida trust, so the result is not certain. The statute is described in Florida community property trusts.

Irrevocable trusts

An irrevocable trust is the document families use when someone is making a completed gift. If the gift is complete and the settlor does not keep the powers that pull the property back into the estate, later growth can sit outside the settlor’s estate. Whether a particular draft does that is read from the powers in that draft.

For 2026, the federal basic exclusion amount is $15 million per individual, and it is indexed afterward. A married couple may have two exclusions. A surviving spouse may also be able to use the unused exclusion of the spouse who died most recently. The estate has to make that election on a timely filed federal estate tax return, even if no tax is owed. The generation-skipping transfer exemption is $15 million per person for 2026, and it is indexed afterward. The annual exclusion for a present-interest gift is $19,000 per donee for 2026, and it is indexed afterward. These federal figures are separate from any Florida homestead exemption. They are not a calculation of tax owed.

A spousal lifetime access trust is an irrevocable trust one spouse creates for the other. The donor makes a completed gift. The beneficiary spouse can receive distributions under the standard in the document. The donor is not a beneficiary. The detail is in spousal lifetime access trusts. Couples who each want a trust should read two SLATs and the reciprocal trust doctrine before anyone signs a matching pair.

An irrevocable life insurance trust is designed to own a life insurance policy. Under section 2042, proceeds are generally included in the estate when they are payable to the estate, or when the insured still holds incidents of ownership, such as the right to change the beneficiary, borrow, or surrender. Premiums the insured pays to the trust are further gifts. A present-interest gift may qualify for the annual exclusion, which is the subject of Crummey letters. A policy the insured already owns, and then transfers, can be included under section 2035 if the insured dies within three years. When the trustee applies for and owns a new policy from the start, the trustee is the original owner of the policy. Read life insurance and the $15 million exemption and why a life insurance policy gets its own trust.

A dynasty trust is a long-term irrevocable trust for children and later generations. Gifts to it can use the basic exclusion and the generation-skipping transfer exemption, which is assigned to the trust on the gift tax return. How long the trust can last depends on the law that governs it.

Pour-over will, power of attorney, and health care directives

These travel with the trust. A pour-over will leaves property that is still in your name at death to the trustee. Property the will has to collect generally passes through probate on the way. The will is also where parents usually nominate a guardian for a minor child.

A durable power of attorney names an agent for property outside the trust, and for tasks a trustee does not perform. Under section 709.2105, a Florida power of attorney is signed by the principal and by two subscribing witnesses, and it is acknowledged before a notary. Under section 732.502, a Florida will is signed by the person who makes it, in the presence of at least two attesting witnesses, who then sign in that person’s presence and in each other’s presence.

A designation of health care surrogate names who speaks to physicians if you cannot. A living will states wishes about life-prolonging procedures. Neither document moves money. Retirement accounts and life insurance pay the person or the trust named on the beneficiary form. The will does not rewrite that form. See naming a trust as IRA beneficiary and the stretch IRA and the 10-year rule.

Questions families ask

Do I need a trust if I already have a will?

A will speaks at death. After a death, the original is deposited with the court clerk and becomes a court record. A funded revocable trust holds property during your life and directs that property at death. Families usually sign both. The will covers what was never moved into the trust, and it is a place to nominate a guardian for a minor child.

Does a living trust avoid probate?

Assets titled in the trustee’s name are administered under the trust, outside a probate of those assets. Assets still in your individual name are generally probate assets in Florida. The pour-over will can send them to the trust after that proceeding. Joint property and assets with a beneficiary form follow those titles and those forms.

Can a trust name who raises my children?

The nomination belongs in the will or in a separate written declaration. A Florida court appoints the guardian of a minor child. The trust is where property for that child can be managed past age 18, on the schedule you set.

What happens to a Florida homestead?

A married owner deeds homestead to a trustee with the spouse’s joinder. At death, if a spouse or a minor child survives, the Florida Constitution limits devise of the homestead. When a minor child survives, a direction in the revocable trust that leaves the house to the spouse does not control the homestead. The descent that follows is in Florida homestead, a spouse, and minor children.

Is a trust something families use only late in life?

Parents use these documents when they have a child, buy a home, or own a business. A guardian nomination matters while children are minors. The same papers are useful at later ages. Irrevocable trusts are the ones that usually involve a gift and the federal exclusion. A revocable living trust is often about title, minors, and who can act.

What is the difference between a revocable trust and an irrevocable trust?

A revocable trust can be changed while you have capacity, and the property generally stays in your estate for federal estate tax. An irrevocable trust is used for a completed gift. The settlor gives up the right to take the property back. A SLAT, a life insurance trust, and a dynasty trust are irrevocable. Each one depends on the powers the document actually gives.
