Florida Real Estate

Florida real estate owned by a person who is not a U.S. person.

Title, homestead, probate, and a transfer into a trust are Florida questions. They are separate from the federal estate tax on U.S.-situs property, and they are separate from the tax and property law of the owner's home country. We advise on the Florida and U.S. side. We do not advise on real property located outside the United States.

The Florida questions that arrive with the deed

A condominium or a house in Florida can be titled in an individual name, in an entity, or in a trustee. Each choice changes probate, homestead, and the federal tax file. Federal withholding on a sale by a foreign person is FIRPTA, which we do not restate here. The trust that may receive the property is discussed on Foreign Trusts.

Whose name is on the deed

A deed into an individual name is simple to sign and expensive to unwind. At death, Florida real property in an individual name is a probate asset in Florida unless a valid transfer has already moved it. An entity or a trustee on the deed avoids that probate only if the entity or the trust actually owns the property and the governing documents are in force. A company that was never funded, or a trust that was signed and never deeded, does not.

Homestead is not the estate-tax exemption

Florida homestead is a state-law status for a natural person who resides in Florida and uses the property as a home. It restricts devise when a spouse or a minor child survives, and a lifetime conveyance by a married owner generally needs the spouse's joinder. It is not the federal estate-tax credit for a nonresident who is not a U.S. citizen. A person who is not a U.S. citizen, and who actually resides in the Florida home, can still have homestead. See Florida homestead and transfers into a trust.

Probate in Florida

If the owner dies holding Florida real property in an individual name, a Florida estate proceeding is the ordinary path to a marketable title, even when a will exists in another country. Ancillary administration is a Florida proceeding. We coordinate it with the fiduciary. We do not administer the estate of the home country.

Funding the property into a trust

A trust is funded when the deed conveys the property to the trustee, in the form the Florida recording statute will accept, and the trust instrument authorizes the trustee to hold it. If the owner is married and the property is homestead, the spouse's joinder is part of that deed. The federal income-tax and estate-tax consequences of the transfer are the tax adviser's to model before the deed is recorded. See funding a foreign trust.

A sale by a foreign person

FIRPTA withholding on a disposition of a U.S. real property interest is a federal regime. We point to it. We do not rewrite it on this page. The English note is FIRPTA, USRPHC, and section 351. The Spanish note on gain is ganancias de capital en la venta de propiedad en Florida.

An LLC is not an estate-tax answer by itself

A Florida or Delaware limited liability company can be a useful title holder. It does not, by its label, decide U.S. estate tax. Income-tax disregarded status does not settle estate-tax situs of a foreign owner's single-member LLC interest. That question is not settled by the income-tax classification. A partnership interest is fact-specific. The Spanish note on that limit is una LLC no decide el impuesto al patrimonio.

Florida law, and the line past it

A pattern we see for families from Mexico, Colombia, or Brazil is a Miami condominium deeded to one person, with a will signed at home and no Florida deed into a trustee. Families from Argentina, Peru, or Panama arrive with the same deed and a different home-country will. The Florida questions are the same: who holds title, whether the owner resides in the property so that homestead applies, what happens if the owner dies, and whether a transfer to a trustee is the right Florida document. Real property in the home country, and the succession law of that country, stay with counsel there. We do not explain those rules.

Federal estate tax on U.S. real property is a separate statute from Florida homestead and from Florida probate. For a decedent who is neither a U.S. citizen nor a U.S. domiciliary, the Code allows a $13,000 unified credit under section 2102(b), which is the credit the Form 706-NA instructions describe against the first $60,000 of the taxable estate, unless a treaty changes the credit. Situs of other asset classes, including deposits, is not the same rule as situs of real property. We do not flatten those classes on this page. The estate-tax engagement is wealth structuring for foreign nationals and U.S.-situs assets inside a foreign trust.
