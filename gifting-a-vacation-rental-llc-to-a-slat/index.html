Gifting a Vacation-Rental LLC to a SLAT

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

A vacation rental the family expects to keep can be placed in a limited liability company, and the units can be given to a spousal lifetime access trust. The gift uses exclusion at today's value. If the gift is complete and the donor keeps no retained enjoyment, later appreciation is in the trust. The SLAT rules are in spousal lifetime access trusts. The partnership and discount statutes, including sections 2036, 2703, and 2704, are discussed without being repeated here in family limited partnerships and trust-owned LPs.

Why the house is in an LLC

The LLC has a manager, a bank account, and an operating agreement that says who sets rent, who approves a sale, and who may transfer units. The trust receives units, not a deed. A deed of Florida homestead is a different transaction, with its own limits, covered in Florida homestead and transfers into a trust and in Florida homestead when a spouse and a minor child survive. A rental the family does not claim as homestead is the property this note is about.

The gift is an assignment of units that the operating agreement permits, at a value an appraiser supports as of the gift date. A non-controlling interest, without the right to force a sale or a redemption, is often worth less than its straight percentage of the property. Sections 2703 and 2704 disregard some restrictions the family can remove. The appraisal is of the interest the agreement actually creates. The longer discussion is in the family-partnership note. This page does not supply a discount.

An illustration: $2 million, then $5 million

The figures in this section are an illustration. They are not a client's property, not an appraisal, and not a forecast. A property worth $2 million today might be worth less in twenty years. The path to $5 million is arithmetic chosen so the subtraction is visible.

Suppose the rental is worth $2 million today, and suppose, only for this illustration, that an appraiser values the gifted non-controlling units at $1.4 million. That $1.4 million is a made-up appraisal. It is not a standard percentage, and it is not a number to put on a return. The gift-tax return would use the real appraisal. Against the 2026 basic exclusion of $15 million per person, indexed afterward, an illustrative $1.4 million gift uses $1.4 million of exclusion.

Twenty years later, in the same illustration, the property is worth $5 million, and the trust still owns the units. If the gift was complete and the donor retained no enjoyment, the estate does not include that $5 million. The amount that stayed outside is the later value, $5 million, against exclusion used of $1.4 million. The $3.6 million difference is growth plus the illustrative discount. If the family had kept the property, the illustration's $5 million would be in the estate. The discount, if a real appraisal supports one, changes how much exclusion the gift consumes on day one. The growth is the point of making the gift before the growth happens.

Use of the house after the gift

Section 2036 includes transferred property if the donor retains the possession or enjoyment of it, or the income from it, or the right to designate who enjoys it. A donor who continues to use the house as a personal residence, or who takes the rents, has a retained enjoyment to explain. A written policy that the LLC follows is the record on the other side: market rent collected for nights the family uses, expenses paid from the LLC account, distributions made as the agreement provides. The tax adviser reads that record. The family-partnership note is where the inclusion statutes are set out at more length.

The beneficiary spouse's right to distributions from the SLAT is a right under that trust. It is not a right in the donor to occupy the house. Personal use runs through the LLC, on the LLC's terms. Basis in the property, if it stays outside the estate, stays a carryover basis. A later swap of the units back into the donor's name is the subject of step-up in basis and the swap power.
