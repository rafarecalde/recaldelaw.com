Directed Trusts: Investment, Distribution, and Administration

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

A directed trust divides the trustee's work. One person directs investments. Another directs distributions. An administrative trustee keeps the records, the custody relationship, and the tax filings, and carries out the directions. Florida codifies that arrangement in the Florida Uniform Directed Trust Act, Part XIV of Chapter 736. The short title is section 736.1401. A trust director holds a power of direction and, in that capacity, is not serving as trustee. Wyoming has its own directed-trust statute, which is one reason families compare the two states in a Wyoming irrevocable trust when you live in Florida.

The directed trustee follows a direction that is within the director's power. The statute leaves the trustee with duties, including a limit where complying would be willful misconduct. The instrument should say, in words a custodian can read, who may give an investment instruction and who may not. An account form that lists the grantor as the sole authorized signer has redesigned the trust.

The investment adviser

The investment adviser directs how the property is invested. The grantor, or a family member who manages the portfolio, can hold this role in many drafts. The limit is whether the power is only an investment power. Section 2036(b) includes stock of a controlled corporation that the decedent transferred and kept the right to vote. A power to direct investments that, in substance, decides who enjoys the property is read under sections 2036 and 2038. Hiring a manager and setting an allocation is one power. Deciding that the family business will not distribute, so that a beneficiary receives nothing, is another.

The distribution adviser

The distribution adviser decides who receives income and principal, within the standard. This is the power the grantor does not hold when the goal is to keep the property out of the estate. A grantor who can sprinkle distributions, or who can distribute to himself or herself, has retained a power sections 2036 and 2038 address.

An ascertainable standard, such as health, education, maintenance, and support, keeps a power from being a general power of appointment when the holder is a beneficiary, under section 2041(b)(1)(A). The same standard does not automatically protect a grantor who holds the power. Section 2041 and sections 2036 and 2038 ask different questions. An independent distribution adviser holds the power. The administrative trustee carries the decision out.

What else the grantor may hold

A power to remove a trustee or a director and to appoint a successor can sit with the grantor if the successor class cannot be the grantor and cannot be someone who will simply do what the grantor says. The tax adviser tests that class. A power to become the distribution trustee is a different draft.

A power to substitute assets of equivalent value, held in a nonfiduciary capacity, is the grantor-trust power in section 675(4)(C). It is covered in step-up in basis and the swap power. Section 736.1405(2)(e) takes the power outside the directed-trust statute when the instrument expressly provides that it is held in a nonfiduciary capacity, as needed for the settlor's tax objectives. The swap power is drafted that way. It is not a distribution power.

The administrative trustee can be an individual, a corporate trustee, or a private family trust company. The Florida family's use of a Wyoming LLC for that seat is in a Wyoming private trust company for a Florida family.

Write as the family, or as the adviser.
