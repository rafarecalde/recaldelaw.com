Spousal Lifetime Access Trusts

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

A spousal lifetime access trust is an irrevocable trust one spouse creates for the other. The donor makes a completed gift. The gift uses the donor's basic exclusion to the extent it exceeds any annual exclusion that actually applies. For 2026 that basic exclusion is $15 million per person, and it is indexed afterward. If the transfer is respected, later growth is outside the donor's estate. The beneficiary spouse can receive distributions under the standard the instrument states. That is the access. It belongs to the beneficiary spouse.

The donor is not a beneficiary. Sections 2036 and 2038 include property the donor transferred and still has a string on: the right to the income or the use of the property, the right to say who enjoys it, or the power to alter, amend, revoke, or terminate the transfer. The instrument is drafted so those strings stay with the trustee and with the standard, not with the donor. The donor does not serve as the trustee who decides distributions to the spouse.

What the spouse's access is

The trustee may distribute income or principal to the beneficiary spouse for health, education, maintenance, and support, or under a broader discretion if an independent trustee holds it. The standard is in the instrument. A pattern of distributions is a fact. An arrangement that the beneficiary spouse will hand the property back to the donor, or will distribute whatever the donor asks, is a retained enjoyment. The tax adviser asks about that before anyone treats the gift as complete for estate tax.

While the spouse can receive income, section 677 often treats the donor as the owner of the trust for income tax. The donor reports the income and pays the tax. That result, and the difference between a gift and a sale to a grantor trust, is covered in selling to an intentionally defective grantor trust. A SLAT is often a grantor trust for that reason. It is still a completed gift if the estate-tax strings are absent.

A gift that can benefit grandchildren may also call for an allocation of generation-skipping transfer exemption. For 2026 that exemption is $15 million, indexed afterward, as stated in the same sale-versus-gift note. The allocation is made on the return.

Divorce

The instrument's definition of spouse decides who the beneficiary is after a divorce. If the trust names a particular person, that person remains a beneficiary unless the instrument says otherwise. A divorce judgment does not rewrite the trust. If the instrument defines spouse as the person to whom the settlor is married at the time of a distribution, the former spouse drops out when the marriage ends. That is a floating spouse clause. A person the settlor later marries can become the spouse-beneficiary under the same definition.

The clause allocates future distributions. It does not divide marital property, and it does not replace the divorce judgment. A limited power of appointment given to "my spouse" follows the definition. A power given to a named person stays with that person. If the former spouse ceases to be a beneficiary, the section 677 reason for grantor-trust status may end, and the trust may become a separate income-tax payer. The tax adviser models that change before the definition is chosen. Families who want each spouse to have a trust face a further question, covered in two SLATs and the reciprocal trust doctrine.

Death

If the beneficiary spouse dies first, the access ends. The trust continues for the descendants or other beneficiaries named in the instrument. A provision that sends the property back to the donor at that death puts the property into the donor's hands, which is a different estate-tax result from the one the gift was made to achieve. The instrument should be read for that default before it is signed.

If the donor dies first, and sections 2036 and 2038 do not apply, the assets remain in the trust. The beneficiary spouse's access can continue. Assets that stay outside the estate do not receive a section 1014 basis adjustment. The way a substitution power can bring a low-basis asset back into the estate is covered in step-up in basis and the swap power. A limited power of appointment in the beneficiary spouse is drafted so it is not a general power of appointment under section 2041. A general power would include the property in that spouse's estate.
