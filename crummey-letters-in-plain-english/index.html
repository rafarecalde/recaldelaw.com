Crummey Letters in Plain English

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

Life insurance premiums have to be paid by the owner of the policy. When the owner is an irrevocable life insurance trust, the money usually comes from the insured, or from the insured's spouse, as a gift to the trust. A gift in trust is a future interest unless someone has a present right to take the money. The annual exclusion applies to a present interest. The withdrawal right, and the letter that tells the beneficiary about it, is how a premium gift is built to qualify.

For 2026 the annual exclusion is $19,000 per donee for a present-interest gift, and it is indexed afterward. That is the figure used in gifting ahead of a liquidity event. A married couple may elect to split gifts, which increases what the couple can give to one person in the year. The election is made on the gift-tax return. The basic exclusion amount, $15 million per person for 2026 and indexed afterward, is the lifetime figure. The annual exclusion sits in front of it, and only for a present interest.

What the letter says

The instrument gives a beneficiary a right to withdraw a stated amount of a contribution, for a stated number of days. The letter tells that beneficiary that a contribution was made, the amount the beneficiary may withdraw, the date the right ends, and how to exercise it. Counsel drafts the letter to the instrument. A form copied from another family's trust describes a right this trust may not give.

The right has to be real. The contribution has to be in the trust during the withdrawal period. The notice has to arrive in time for the beneficiary to act. A letter sent after the window closed describes a right that already ended. The trustee keeps a copy of the letter and a record of how it was sent. That file is what the tax adviser has if the return is asked about.

Thirty days is a common period in instruments. It is a drafting choice. The Code does not fix the number of days. The period has to be long enough that the right is a real one.

When the beneficiary is a minor

A minor cannot practically read the letter and demand the money. The instrument names an adult, other than the donor, who may exercise the right for the child. Notice goes to that adult. A notice addressed only to the child leaves the right with someone who cannot use it. The donor is a poor sole holder of the child's right: the person who made the gift should not be the only person who can waive it or let it lapse.

The adult's job is to decide, for the child, whether to withdraw. In a premium trust the family usually wants the money left in place so the trustee can pay the carrier. The decision is still a real one. An understanding that no one will ever withdraw, if it is firm enough that the right is a fiction, is a fact the tax adviser will want to know about before treating the gift as a present interest.

What happens when the right lapses

If the beneficiary does not withdraw, the right lapses. Section 2514(e) treats a lapse of a general power as a release, to the extent the property exceeds the greater of $5,000 or 5 percent of the trust assets. A release can be a gift by the beneficiary to the people who take what is left. Instruments deal with that arithmetic by limiting the withdrawal right, or by letting the excess hang so that only the 5-and-5 amount lapses in the year. The tax adviser checks the lapse against that year's contributions. The letter does not do that math by itself.

The same notices are how premiums reach the trust described in life insurance and the $15 million exemption and in why the policy has its own trust. A large gift of investments, of the kind in the liquidity-event note, is usually a future interest and uses the basic exclusion. It is a different gift, with a different return.

Write as the family, or as the adviser.
