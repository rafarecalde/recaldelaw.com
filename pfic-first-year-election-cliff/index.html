Fund Formation & Cross-Border Structuring

The First-Year Cliff

By Rafael Recalde — November 12, 2026

In this series. The election discussed in the tiering note is due for the first year the position is held. A cash-sleeve PFIC starts that year when it is bought. Keeping the lower tier out of corporate classification is a classification decision, not a filing shortcut. Dealing dates, as an operational matter, are discussed in Open-Ended vs. Closed-Ended Cayman Funds. That note is about exit rights. The practice page is Cayman funds.

A qualified electing fund election is made for the first year of the holding, or the holding carries the excess-distribution regime of section 1291, with its interest charge, until a purging election. For an open-ended fund that adds portfolio positions during the year, the rule is an operating constraint. Each new foreign corporation can start a new first year. The election for that corporation is due with the return for the year of the subscription.

The due date

Treasury Regulation section 1.1295-1(e) provides that, except as the retroactive-consent regulation provides, the shareholder makes the section 1295 election on or before the due date, including extensions, of the shareholder's income tax return for the first taxable year to which the election will apply. The election is made on the original return for that year, or on an amended return filed on or before the same due date. For a position acquired during the year, that year is the year of acquisition.

The Instructions for Form 8621 (December 2025) state the coordination with section 1291. If the qualified electing fund election is not made for the first year of the shareholder's holding period in the PFIC, the shareholder may be able to make a deemed-sale election or, if eligible, a deemed-dividend election. A deemed-sale election under section 1291(d)(2)(A), which the instructions describe, treats the shareholder as having sold the stock at fair market value on the first day of the PFIC's first taxable year as a qualified electing fund. The instructions state that the gain is taxed as an excess distribution received on that date, and that after the deemed sale the PFIC becomes a pedigreed qualified electing fund with respect to the shareholder. The deemed-dividend election is available only where the shareholder is eligible. We do not set out those eligibility conditions, and we do not describe either election as a routine correction. Each is a recognition event. The tax adviser models it.

Section 1.1295-1(e) points to a separate regulation for a retroactive election made with consent. That consent is not the ordinary due date. We do not describe the request.

A year of corporate status stays on the holding

Section 1298(b)(1) provides that stock is treated as stock in a PFIC if, at any time during the taxpayer's holding period, the corporation was a PFIC that was not a qualified electing fund. The shareholder can elect to recognize gain, as of the last day of the last taxable year for which the company was a PFIC, under rules similar to section 1291(d)(2). A later qualified electing fund election does not, by being filed the next year, erase the earlier year.

An open-ended fund that subscribes to new portfolio funds on its dealing dates can add, with each subscription, a foreign corporation and a holding period. The election year for that corporation is the year that includes the acquisition. It is not the year in which the annual accounts happen to be assembled. Dealing dates, valuation dates, and payment dates are three different dates. How to read them in a fund's documents is the subject of the note on open-ended and closed-ended funds. That discussion is about investor exit rights. It is not advice on the tax election. The only connection is the calendar: positions arrive during the year, and the election year is the year they arrive.

This is a practical reason to keep lower-tier vehicles out of corporate classification where the classification regulations and the organizing law actually allow it. It is not a reason that overrides those limits. A partnership default depends on the liability clause. An election after classification is relevant is a deemed liquidation and contribution. A third-party corporate fund the manager does not control cannot be reclassified from the outside.

What we leave to the computation

Section 1291 imposes the excess-distribution regime, including an interest charge, on the terms of that section. We do not restate the computational formula, and we do not illustrate it with a rate or an amount. The tax adviser computes it for the holder who is in the regime. The structural point is the one above: the first year is the year the position is acquired, and a purging election is a recognition event rather than a filing that puts the clock back.
