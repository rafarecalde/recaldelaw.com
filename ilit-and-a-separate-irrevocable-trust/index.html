Why a Life Insurance Policy Gets Its Own Trust

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

Families who already have an irrevocable trust for investments sometimes ask why the life insurance needs a second one. The policy can sit in the broader trust. Often it should not. An irrevocable life insurance trust owns the contract, or the LLC that owns the contract, and little else. The broader trust holds the portfolio, the business interest, or the rental company, and it may give a spouse access. Those are different jobs, and the powers that make the broader trust useful are the powers that can pull a policy back into the estate.

Incidents of ownership follow the person who can act

Section 2042 includes the proceeds when the insured possesses incidents of ownership at death. The insured of a policy inside a trust is in that position if he or she can change the beneficiary, borrow, assign, or surrender, including as trustee. The usual ILIT names a trustee who is not the insured. The insured is not given a role over the contract.

A broader trust is often drafted so the grantor can do something: direct investments, swap assets of equal value, or remove a trustee. Some of those powers are compatible with keeping the trust property out of the estate. They are a poor fit for a policy on the grantor's own life. A swap power that lets the insured take the policy back is a power over the contract. The basis note on the substitution power is about marketable assets and real estate interests. It is not an invitation to put a swap power over the insured's own policy into the same instrument. Revenue Ruling 2011-28 is the ruling the tax adviser reads when a substitution power touches a policy.

Access is the other reason to separate them

A spousal lifetime access trust exists so the beneficiary spouse can receive distributions. That access is the point, and it is described in SLATs. If the policy is inside that trust, the access reaches the contract. A distribution for the spouse's support can become a distribution that strips cash value. A divorce clause that redirects the SLAT can redirect the policy with it. A creditor of the investment trust, or a bad year in the portfolio, can put the contract in a trust that now needs cash.

The ILIT's beneficiaries may overlap the broader trust's beneficiaries. The overlap is in the people, not in the powers. Premium gifts are sized to the premium and are paired with withdrawal notices, covered in Crummey letters. A gift of an investment portfolio is sized to the basic exclusion and usually has no withdrawal right of that kind. Mixing the two gifts in one instrument makes both harder to administer and harder to explain on a return.

The carrier and the estate's liquidity

The insurance company wants one owner, one beneficiary designation, and a person who can sign. A trust whose only asset is the policy, or the membership interest in the LLC that holds the policy, is a short application. The LLC route, and the requirement that the insured spouse keep no role in that company, is covered in moving a PPLI policy from an LLC into an ILIT. The same ownership point applies to an ordinary policy.

After the death, the proceeds are in the ILIT. If the estate needs cash for tax or for a buyout, the instrument can authorize the trustee to lend to the estate or to buy assets from the estate. The loan is documented as a loan. The purchase is at a value the tax adviser can support. That liquidity is available because the proceeds were outside the estate under section 2042, which is the counting exercise in life insurance and the $15 million exemption.
