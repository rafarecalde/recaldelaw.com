Two SLATs and the Reciprocal Trust Doctrine

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law. What follows is general. It describes no family's facts and no family's numbers. Tax results are confirmed with the family's tax adviser. We advise on the legal structure.

One SLAT uses one spouse's exclusion and leaves the other spouse with access. A couple that wants both exclusions used, and each spouse protected if the other dies first, often signs two. Each spouse is the donor of one trust and a beneficiary of the other. The single-trust rules are in spousal lifetime access trusts. The additional question is whether the two trusts are, in substance, one exchange.

What the doctrine looks at

The reciprocal trust doctrine is the name for a substance-over-form reading. Where the trusts are interrelated, and where they leave each spouse in about the same economic position as if each had created a trust for himself or herself, the Service can treat each spouse as the settlor of the trust the other created. Estate inclusion can follow, under the same retained-interest provisions that a single SLAT is drafted to avoid, sections 2036 and 2038. It is a way of reading two instruments together, and the Code states no safe harbor for it. The tax adviser applies it to the pair of drafts.

Mirror image trusts, signed together, funded with equal value, and giving each spouse the same standard and the same power, are the pattern the doctrine describes. A small wording change that leaves the economics the same is a thin record.

Differences that are real

Timing. The trusts are signed and funded at different times. There is no statutory waiting period and no number of days that guarantees a result. A gap that is real, across different decisions and often different tax periods, is a fact. Two ceremonies on the same afternoon are a different fact.

Assets. The trusts are funded with different property, in different amounts. One may hold a portfolio. The other may hold units in a rental company, as in gifting a vacation-rental LLC to a SLAT. Equal value on the same day is the pattern to avoid. Different values also mean the two gifts use different amounts of the $15 million basic exclusion. For 2026 that exclusion is $15 million per person, indexed afterward.

Distribution standards. One instrument may limit distributions to health, education, maintenance, and support. The other may give an independent trustee a broader discretion, or a different class of additional beneficiaries. The standards should describe different powers, not the same power with a synonym.

Powers of appointment. One beneficiary spouse may hold a lifetime limited power to appoint among descendants. The other may hold only a testamentary power, or a power that includes charity, or no power. A limited power is drafted so it is not a general power under section 2041. Giving both spouses the same power on the same terms does not help the pair. Giving one spouse a general power would include the property in that spouse's estate, which is a high price for a difference.

Trustees can differ as well. A different name, with the same powers and the same family relationship, adds less than a different standard or a different power of appointment.

Each trust still has to work alone

A difference between the trusts does not repair a string inside one of them. Each donor still avoids retaining the income, the use, and the power to change beneficiaries. Each donor is still outside the distribution role. An implied understanding that each beneficiary spouse will distribute to the donor on request is the same retained-enjoyment fact as in a single SLAT, doubled.

Income tax is a separate reading. While a spouse can receive income, section 677 often makes the donor of that trust the income-tax owner. The reciprocal-trust question is an estate-tax question. The tax adviser reads both, on both instruments, before either is funded. The generation-skipping exemption, if grandchildren can benefit, is allocated on the return for each trust that needs it.
