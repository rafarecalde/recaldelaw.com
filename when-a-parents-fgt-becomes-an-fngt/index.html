Cross-Border, International & Wealth Structuring

When a Parent's FGT Becomes an FNGT

By Rafael Recalde — November 22, 2026

Recalde Law Firm, P.A. is a Florida law firm with an office in Miami, Florida. We advise on United States law only. We do not practice Mexican law, and we do not give Mexican tax or succession advice. We do not practice the law of any other country. Formation of a non-U.S. entity, and the local law of that jurisdiction, are matters for local counsel. We handle the U.S. architecture: classification, the documents a U.S. custodian and a U.S. court will read, and coordination with the family's tax adviser.

Families experience this as a wire that used to be simple. While the parent was alive, money moved from the trust to a U.S. child and the U.S. adviser treated it as a gift from the parent. After the parent dies, the same wire can be a distribution from a foreign non-grantor trust, and the income that was left inside can come with a throwback computation. The change is classification. It happens because the facts that made the trust a foreign grantor trust were facts about the parent.

The longer note on the moment of death is when the foreign grantor dies. The computation is in the throwback rule. This note is the sequence an RIA or a family office walks through when the parent was the foreign grantor and the children are U.S. persons. It is not a return. Mexican succession and Mexican tax stay with Mexican counsel.

What was true the day before

Section 672(f) allows a foreign grantor to be the income-tax owner in narrow cases: a power to revest exercisable by the grantor alone, or a trust that during the grantor's life distributes only to the grantor or the grantor's spouse. While that status holds, the trust's income is the parent's. A transfer to a U.S. child is generally a gift from the foreign parent. If it crosses the threshold, it is reported on the foreign-gift portion of Form 3520. It is not an accumulation distribution. The two portions of the form are easy to mix, and they are not interchangeable. See U.S. beneficiaries of foreign trusts.

The day the parent dies

The power to revoke dies with the parent. A limit that distributions may be made to the parent only during the parent's life closes on the same day. If the trust remains foreign under section 7701, and if no other person is treated as the owner under sections 671 through 679, the trust is a foreign non-grantor trust. It is its own taxpayer. Income it receives after death is its income.

Distributable net income is the measure of current-year income that a distribution can carry out. Income of a prior year that was not distributed is undistributed net income under section 665. A distribution to a U.S. child that stays within the current year's distributable net income carries out that current income. A distribution above it is an accumulation distribution of undistributed net income. Sections 665 through 668 then apply. The tax is the throwback computation. Section 668 adds an interest charge for the years the income was accumulated, using the underpayment rate. This note does not quote a flat percentage. The rate and the arithmetic are the tax adviser's, on the year's facts.

The trustee's choice in the first years is whether post-death income is distributed currently or left in the trust. Leaving it in, including leaving it in because the account is frozen and nobody signs, is accumulation. A beneficiary statement that separates current income from corpus is what lets the U.S. child report a current distribution as a current distribution. Without that statement, the default treatment is the one the throwback rules apply to an accumulation distribution.

The form changes with the classification

After death, a payment from the trust to a U.S. child is reported as a trust distribution, not as a gift from the parent. A bequest from the parent's estate can still be a foreign gift or bequest if it crosses the threshold. The estate and the trust are different payors, even in the same month. Form 3520-A is the return of a foreign trust with a U.S. owner. An FNGT with no U.S. owner does not start that return merely because a U.S. child is a beneficiary. The filing map is in Forms 3520 and 3520-A.

What domestication does, and what it leaves in place

A trust that becomes domestic at death, on the pattern in a foreign grantor trust that becomes a Wyoming dynasty trust, is written so that post-death income is earned by a domestic trust. Income earned while the trust is domestic is outside the foreign throwback rule. Undistributed net income from any period in which the trust was a foreign non-grantor trust can still sit in the trust. A later distribution of that older pool to a U.S. child can still be an accumulation distribution. The adviser should not describe domestication as a wipe of the account history.

Estate tax remains separate. U.S.-situs assets can be in the nonresident parent's gross estate because of a retained power, with the limited exemption from the section 2102(b) credit. Foreign-situs assets generally are not. Income-tax classification at death does not decide that inventory. The parent's U.S. estate-tax file is not computed with the $15 million basic exclusion, which is the 2026 figure for a U.S. person, indexed afterward.
