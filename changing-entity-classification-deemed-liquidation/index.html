Fund Formation & Cross-Border Structuring

Changing Classification Is Not Free

By Rafael Recalde — November 12, 2026

In this series. Paragraph (g) of the classification regulations is the price of an elective change. The seventy-five-day window can date that election. It does not repeal the deemed transactions. The corporate classification this note unwinds is the one the liability clause may already have produced. The practice page is Cayman funds.

An elective change from a corporation to a partnership is not a new label on the same entity. Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3(g) treats it as a liquidation followed by a contribution. That is the path the regulations describe, and they describe it only for an election. Amending the liability clause after classification is relevant is not a silent version of the same path. Under paragraph (a), that amendment does not change the classification.

What an elective change is deemed to be

Section 301.7701-3(g)(1) states four elective changes.

If an association elects to be a partnership, the association is treated as distributing all of its assets and liabilities to its shareholders in liquidation, and immediately thereafter the shareholders are treated as contributing those assets and liabilities to a newly formed partnership.

If a partnership elects to be an association, the partnership is treated as contributing all of its assets and liabilities to the association for stock, and immediately thereafter as liquidating by distributing that stock to its partners.

If an association elects to be disregarded, it is treated as distributing all of its assets and liabilities to its single owner in liquidation. If a disregarded entity elects to be an association, the owner is treated as contributing all of the entity's assets and liabilities to the association for stock.

Paragraph (g)(3) times the steps. The election is treated as occurring at the start of the day it is effective. The deemed transactions are treated as occurring immediately before the close of the day before. An election effective on January 1 is reported by the owners as a December 31 event. The association's last day is December 31. The partnership's first day is January 1.

Paragraph (g)(2) provides that the tax treatment of an elective change is determined under all relevant provisions of the Internal Revenue Code and general principles of tax law, including the step-transaction doctrine. The deemed steps are not a representation that the liquidation is tax-free, and they are not a representation of any particular amount of gain. The result depends on the assets, the bases, and the owner chart. Cross-border consequences of a deemed liquidation or a deemed contribution depend on who the owners are. The tax adviser models that. We do not state a result.

An amendment is a different act

Each rule in paragraph (g)(1) begins with an election. The heading of paragraph (g) is elective changes in classification. Section 301.7701-3(a) addresses the other route directly: an entity whose classification was determined under the default retains that classification regardless of changes in the members' liability while the classification is relevant, until it elects to change.

An amendment that removes limited liability, after relevance, can change who is exposed under the organizing law, if local counsel confirms that the amendment does that. The regulations do not deem a liquidation on that amendment. They say the U.S. classification stays where it was. There is no deemed transaction to analyze, because there is no change in classification, until someone files the election. At that point paragraph (g) applies, with the consequences above.

There is a narrow reset, and it is not an amendment regime. Section 301.7701-3(d)(3) provides that if a foreign eligible entity's classification is not relevant for sixty consecutive months, the classification is initially determined again under the foreign default rules when it next becomes relevant. A fund whose classification affects a U.S. person's federal tax or information liability will ordinarily be relevant throughout. We state the reset so it is not confused with paragraph (g). We do not describe it as a plan for taking a classification off the table.

The owner chart comes before the form

Before an election is filed, the useful list is who owns the vehicle, directly and through the tiers, and which of those persons are U.S. persons. The signature rule for a change reaches owners on the date the deemed transactions occur. The tax consequences of the deemed liquidation, whatever the tax adviser computes them to be, are consequences for the persons treated as receiving the distribution. Knowing that list after the election is filed is late.

A corporate classification is also what puts a securities fund into the PFIC analysis. Changing it in order to collapse lower-tier reporting positions is the subject of classifying lower-tier funds as partnerships. The deemed transactions in this note are part of that decision. They are not a clerical step on the way to a single Form 8621.
