The Seventy-Five Day Window Nobody Uses Well

By Rafael Recalde — November 12, 2026

In this series. The window is a rule about the effective date of Form 8832. It assumes the classification decision has been made. The liability clause is what the default will be if the election is late. A change inside the window is still a deemed transaction if it changes a classification that has already applied. The practice page is Cayman funds.

A classification election can name an effective date up to seventy-five days before it is filed. Used against a closing that has already happened, and that is still inside the window, the classification can be fixed as of the day it became relevant even though the paper went out later. Used as a reason to decide the classification after the fund is launched, the same rule is how a fund spends its first stretch as a corporation and then locks the correction for sixty months.

The rule, including the part that is not optional

Treasury Regulation section 301.7701-3(c)(1)(iii) provides that an election is effective on the date specified on Form 8832, or on the date filed if no date is specified. The specified date cannot be more than seventy-five days before the date the election is filed, and it cannot be more than twelve months after that date. If the form specifies a date more than seventy-five days before filing, the election is effective seventy-five days before the date it was filed. If it specifies a date more than twelve months after filing, the election is effective twelve months after the date it was filed. The election is not rejected for naming a date outside the range. The regulation moves the effective date to the edge of the range.

The instructions published with Form 8832 (Rev. December 2013) state the same limits, and they state the same adjustment when line 8 names a date outside them. Seventy-five days is the figure in the current regulation and on the form the Internal Revenue Service is publishing.

What the window is for

The regulation's own example is a foreign eligible entity whose members all have limited liability, so the default is an association. Its classification becomes relevant when a U.S. person acquires an interest. To be classified as a partnership on that date, the entity must file Form 8832 within seventy-five days. Filed later, it is an association from the date of relevance until the election's effective date, and the election that then changes the classification is subject to the sixty-month limitation in paragraph (c)(1)(iv).

That is the use. A subscription, or another event that makes classification relevant, can be followed by the filing, and the effective date can still be the event, if the filing is inside seventy-five days. The window is not a standing extension of the decision. Relevance is also not limited to the arrival of a subscription dollar. Paragraph (d)(1) treats classification as relevant when it affects any person's federal tax or information liability, and the regulation gives withholding and an information-return obligation as examples.

What the window does not do

It does not hold the default open between formation and the first U.S. investor. When classification becomes relevant, section 301.7701-3(d)(2) determines the initial classification from the liability regime then in effect, unless an election is effective as of that time. Waiting to see who subscribes, and filing outside the window, produces a period of corporate classification. The election that ends that period is a change, and paragraph (c)(1)(iv) then limits a further elective change for sixty months. An election that is effective on the date of formation is not a change for that purpose. Getting there requires the decision at formation, and a filing whose effective date is the formation date, not a filing invented after the fact.

The form instructions describe a separate late-election procedure, Revenue Procedure 2009-41, with its own conditions, including reasonable cause, and a period measured as three years and seventy-five days from the requested effective date. Where that procedure does not apply, the instructions point to a private letter ruling. That relief is not the seventy-five-day rule, and it is not automatic. The tax adviser determines whether it is available on the returns that have been filed. It is not a backup plan for a decision deferred at formation.

A timely filing that changes a classification already in effect still carries the deemed transactions in section 301.7701-3(g). The window dates the election. It does not make a change free. If the desired classification is the default, the instructions say not to file Form 8832 at all.
